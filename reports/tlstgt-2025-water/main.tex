\begin{abstract}
This report audits the principal numerical and mechanistic claims of a
graph-transformer attack detector for water distribution systems. It is not
solely a reproduction attempt. Reproduction alone is a weak instrument: it
invites the reply that the implementation was at fault, an objection that was
correct at one point during this audit and is documented as such. The audit
therefore proceeds in tiers, beginning with whether the reported numbers cohere
internally, then whether the detection task is non-trivial at all, then whether
each claimed component contributes, and only finally whether the headline
pattern reproduces. Every experiment is designed so that its outcome is not
controlled by the expected answer, and results that disconfirm the audit's own
expectations are reported as such. Conclusions are bounded to the artefact: this
report makes no claim about author intent.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and testable claims}

The target paper reports F1, accuracy, and detection rate for nine detectors
across three graph sizes (10, 20, 31 nodes) of the C-Town water network, and
claims (i) a monotone improvement with graph size, (ii) a large margin for the
proposed spatio-temporal graph transformer (STGT) over conventional baselines,
(iii) a further gain from transfer learning across graph sizes, and (iv) a
mechanism: that spatial and temporal dependencies are too difficult for a
feed-forward network to capture, which is why a graph convolution and a
transformer are required.

Claim (iv) is testable independently of whether any number reproduces, and is
treated here as the primary target. Claims (i)--(iii) are evaluated against
distributions across seeds rather than point estimates, because measured
run-to-run spread for these architectures is large relative to the margins
claimed.

\section{Source protocol reconstruction}

Benign readings are the canonical DeepH2O C-Town SCADA series (31 sensors:
7 tank levels, 11 pump flows, 1 valve flow, 12 junction pressures). Malicious
samples are produced by modifying benign readings with three printed equations:
replay ($X^m_t = X^b_{t-\Delta t}$), denial of service ($X^m_t = X^b_{t-1}$,
sustained), and data manipulation ($X^m_t = X^b_t + \delta$,
$-5 \le \delta \le 5$ in steps of $0.2$). Detection compares a one-step forecast
against the observed reading, scores the residual by squared Mahalanobis
distance against a benign Gaussian, averages over consecutive batches of size
$S$, and thresholds using the validation set.

The reduced 10- and 20-node graphs are transcribed verbatim from the paper's
Figure~1 rather than recomputed. This matters: the paper names betweenness
centrality as the reduction criterion, but a betweenness ranking retains node
J422 and drops PU8, whereas Figure~1(a) retains PU8. The printed figure governs.
Transcribed node sets are nested and their induced edges match the drawing
exactly (12, 24, and 44 edges).

\section{Method}

All experiments run on a single cluster environment with fixed library versions;
each job records SHA-256 of code and data and runs a regression suite as a
preflight. Zero-parameter reference detectors are evaluated through the
\emph{identical} scoring, threshold, and metric path as every trained model, so
any difference is attributable to the model rather than to the evaluation.
Comparisons between detectors are made at a matched false-alarm rate; an earlier
draft of this audit compared detectors at different operating points and
overstated the trained models' advantage as a result.

Ambiguities in the printed method are enumerated and pre-registered before
results are examined, covering training-set class balance, the units of
$\delta$, the detection batch size, the threshold rule, the transformer
placement, and the population used for the Mahalanobis fit.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Internal consistency of the reported table}

On the paper's own stated balanced split, accuracy satisfies
$\mathrm{ACC} = (\mathrm{DR} + \mathrm{SP})/2$ with $\mathrm{SP} \le 100\%$, so
$\mathrm{ACC} \le (\mathrm{DR}+100)/2$. Twenty-four of twenty-seven reported
cells exceed this ceiling. Applying the stricter joint check, which uses all
three reported metrics, all twenty-seven fail.

A stronger test asks whether each reported triple is attainable from \emph{any}
integer confusion matrix on a balanced test set. Scanning per-class sizes from
70 (the size implied by the paper's own description of 1{,}400 hours split
80/10/10) to 700, no cell is attainable at any size. The checker was validated
against fifteen constructed confusion matrices and accepted all fifteen.

The deviation is systematic rather than random. Of the twenty-five cells where
the joint identity is evaluable, twenty-five report an accuracy above what their
own detection rate and F1 imply, and none below, with a mean inflation of
$+30.6$ points (maximum $+54.7$). A sign test against chance gives
$p = 6\times10^{-8}$. Isolated transcription errors and rounding are therefore
excluded, as these would scatter in both directions.

\subsection{Does any measurement protocol reproduce the table?}

Showing that numbers are unattainable under the protocol a paper \emph{describes}
is escapable: a defender may argue the description was inaccurate. The stronger
test asks whether \emph{any} protocol reproduces them. Formally, under
$H_0$: ``the reported triple arose from a real confusion matrix under some
protocol'', we evaluate $P(\text{table} \mid H_0)$ across a pre-registered space
of 67{,}326 protocols per cell --- every test-set size from 20 to 1{,}000, every
class prevalence from 2\% to 98\%, and six metric-definition variants including
balanced accuracy, detection-rate/precision swaps, negative-class and macro F1,
and specificity reported in the detection-rate column.

Twenty of the twenty-seven cells are unreachable under \emph{every} protocol
searched. For those cells the likelihood under $H_0$ is exactly zero rather than
small. Seven cells are individually reachable under some exotic setting, but the
decisive constraint is joint: a paper has one protocol, not one per row. The best
single protocol explains \textbf{one} cell of twenty-seven, and \textbf{no}
protocol explains the table.

The searcher was validated by constructing confusion matrices at several sizes
and prevalences, rounding their metrics, and confirming recovery in every case.

\subsection{Is the task non-trivial?}

Two zero-parameter detectors were evaluated: a per-sensor $z$-score on the
observed reading, and persistence (predict the next reading as the last observed
reading). Both achieve F1 $\approx 45$ at a false-alarm rate below $8\%$. Every
baseline the paper reports --- SVM (6.1), RF (17.5), LGBM (19.8), FFNN (20.9),
AE (21.5), LSTM (26.0) at ten nodes --- falls \emph{below} a detector with no
parameters.

Decomposing by attack class reveals why. Table~\ref{tab:classes} shows that the
task is not one problem but three, with sharply different difficulty.

A composite of the two trivial rules ($z$-score or stuck-sensor) achieves
F1 71.5, accuracy 76.9, and detection rate 60.5 at a false-alarm rate of 7.9\%,
with zero parameters and no seed variance. Trained models reach F1 $\approx 70$
at a \emph{higher} false-alarm rate when trained on benign windows only, and
only 44--46 under the paper's own stated prescription that every split carry
equal samples per class. Under the paper's literal training reading, every
trained model scores below the zero-parameter composite.

\subsection{Is replay detectable at all?}

Replay reproduces genuine past readings, so a replayed window is itself a real
benign window. Measuring nearest-neighbour distance from held-out test windows
to a bank of attack-free training windows, replay is indistinguishable from
normal data (AUC 0.455, median distance 9.75 versus 9.92 for normal), while
denial of service (AUC 0.917) and manipulation (AUC 0.988) separate clearly.
The replay ceiling is therefore a property of the data, not of any detector.

With replay undetectable, the achievable detection rate is bounded near
$1 - (\text{replay share})$, which is 57.1\% under the attack mix used here,
against a reported 74.7\% for STGT and 76.8\% with transfer learning. The paper
does not state its attack mix, so this bound is conditional on the mix rather
than absolute.

\subsection{Is the feed-forward network capacity-limited?}

The paper attributes its architecture to the difficulty of spatio-temporal
structure for a feed-forward network. Varying feed-forward capacity across a
272-fold parameter range (19{,}681 to 5{,}347{,}031 parameters) leaves
performance flat: AUC 0.790, 0.785, 0.761, 0.787 and detection rate 56.7, 56.9,
41.9, 56.2 at a matched false-alarm rate. A two-layer network with fewer than
twenty thousand parameters matches both the largest network and STGT.

Structure does exist in the data --- mean cross-sensor correlation 0.18--0.24
and lag-one temporal correlation 0.65--0.74 on attack-free windows --- so the
premise that structure is present is not empty. What fails is the inference that
capturing it requires the proposed architecture.

\subsection{Where the paper's premise holds}

Reported because it disconfirms this audit's expectation. Shuffling the C-Town
adjacency, or removing its edges entirely, degrades performance by up to 18 F1
points: STGT at ten nodes scores 57.9 with the real topology, 51.9 shuffled, and
44.9 with no edges; TL-STGT at 31 nodes scores 63.6, 45.2, and 44.7 respectively.
The graph is not decorative.

Transfer learning likewise shows a large gain at 31 nodes, $+13.3$ to $+21.9$ F1
over training from scratch. Two qualifications apply. The comparison is
budget-confounded, since the transferred model is pretrained on the smaller
graphs and sees roughly three times the total training. And under benign-only
training the gain collapses to $+3.1$, indicating that transfer mainly buys
robustness to the paper's attack-corrupted training prescription rather than
better detection.

\subsection{Configuration dominates model choice}

The paper states that every split carries an equal number of samples per class,
so half of the training data is attack-corrupted. Training instead on benign
windows changes F1 at 31 nodes by roughly 25 points: 44.1 versus 70.3 for the
feed-forward network, 46.0 versus 71.2 for the recurrent baseline, 44.6 versus
70.8 for the graph baseline, 46.1 versus 67.8 for the proposed model, and 68.0
versus 70.9 with transfer learning. Every figure in this report therefore states
its configuration; an unattributed value is meaningless at that sensitivity.

\subsection{Stability}

Across five seeds on identical hardware, software, and data, TGCN spans 23.7 F1
points and STGT 11.8, while feed-forward and recurrent baselines remain within
2--8. STGT's denial-of-service detection alone swings from 5.8\% to 95.8\%. The
paper reports single values to one decimal place with no dispersion.

\section{Corrections to this audit}

Two earlier conclusions of this audit were wrong and are retracted. First, a
reported 46-point shortfall against the paper was substantially caused by a
defect in our own threshold selection, which drew reference samples from
validation windows contaminated by adjacent attacks and inflated the threshold
by roughly four orders of magnitude; corrected, the shortfall is far smaller.
Second, an identical-metrics collapse across architectures was attributed to the
scale incoherence of $\delta$; the corrected runs show that the threshold defect
alone produced it. Third, an earlier draft quoted benign-training results without
stating the configuration, while the paper's literal reading trains on the
balanced split; the two differ by roughly 25 F1. Fourth, this audit expected the
graph topology to carry no signal, and it does. All are recorded in the evidence
log rather than silently amended.

\section{Limitations}

The benign series is the canonical DeepH2O C-Town series rather than the paper's
own unpublished 1{,}400-hour run. Attack duration, the replay offset
$\Delta t$, the sliding-window length, and the detection rule for the shallow
classifiers are not specified by the paper and are documented assumptions. The
attack mix is ours, which conditions the detection-rate ceiling. The
interpretation sweep over pre-registered ambiguity axes is incomplete.

\section{Verdict}

Bounded to the artefact and to the finite space tested: the reported table is
not reproducible by any correct implementation. No confusion matrix on the
described test set yields the printed numbers; the deviation from internal
consistency is systematic rather than accidental; and an exhaustive search over
67{,}326 measurement protocols per cell finds no single protocol that reproduces
the table, with twenty of twenty-seven cells unreachable under every protocol
searched. Independently of the
numbers, the claimed mechanism does not hold: the detection task decomposes into
one class requiring no model, one class that the paper's own detector cannot
express but a two-line rule solves, and one class that is undetectable in
principle. A composite of two zero-parameter rules matches the proposed
architecture at a lower false-alarm rate and with no seed variance.

This report does not assert how the reported numbers arose. Establishing that no
measurement process in the searched space produces them does not distinguish
deliberate fabrication from catastrophic process failure --- placeholder values
never replaced, or a table carried over from a draft --- since both yield numbers
that were never measured. That distinction requires evidence outside the paper,
which this report does not have and does not claim.
