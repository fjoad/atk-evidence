Research reproduction notes

Can we reproduce the published results?

We read a paper, implement the method it describes, and compare our results with the published ones. When they differ, we try to understand why.

These pages show that work: the instructions we followed, the choices we had to make, the code we ran, and the evidence for each conclusion. A successful reproduction matters just as much as an unsuccessful one.

A follow-up found a stronger, conditional limit. On these prepared inputs, even a detector allowed to choose each Softmax reconstruction using the true label stays below 50.93% balanced accuracy, versus 83% reported. At at most 15% false alarms, detection cannot exceed 9.25%, versus 81%. New weights or seeds cannot fix that gap while the prepared data, output restriction, and mean-squared-error score stay the same.

This does not mean the model does nothing useful: its fitted score makes small beneficial changes and ranks attacks better than the simple controls tested within similar-input-magnitude groups. Different preprocessing, output layers, and scores remain outside the bound. Read the calculation and its limits.

How we investigate a paper

Read and rebuild

Start with the complete paper. Connect each important instruction to the code, and say where the text leaves a choice.

Test and compare

Try small checks first. Run the stated experiment, keep the outputs, and compare every reported measure—not just the best one.

Explain the difference

Test possible explanations. Ask what training adds, whether the claimed component matters, and what limits the method can have.

We distinguish three questions: Did the result reproduce? Did the method work for the reason claimed? Could the stated method reach the target? One failed run cannot answer all three. A mathematical limit must state its assumptions; a statistical conclusion must state what was sampled.

Small exploratory experiments help choose the next question. They do not become reproductions just because their results are interesting. We report our own mistakes and preserve earlier attempts.
