Graph transfer learning for water-network attack detection

Ahasan, Joad, Atat, Thompson, Serpedin, Takiddin — EUSIPCO 2025.

Earlier study, retained with its corrections. Our saved arithmetic search tested 67,326 combinations of test size, class balance, and metric definition per cell. None explained the whole reported table; 20 of 27 cells had no match anywhere in that finite search. This is a statement about the combinations tested, not every imaginable evaluation procedure.

The search used 229 selected test sizes between 20 and 1,000, 49 class prevalences, and six metric definitions. Read the saved search output. No water-network experiment was rerun for this editorial update.

Disclosure. One of this project’s maintainers is a co-author of this paper. The study is therefore not independent of the paper’s authors. Its assumptions, measurements, and our own mistakes are recorded below. This page does not assert how the reported numbers arose.

1. Checking whether the numbers fit together

On the paper’s own balanced 50/50 split, ACC = (DR + SP)/2 with SP ≤ 100%, hence ACC ≤ (DR+100)/2.

All 25 evaluable differences point in the same direction. The quoted sign-test probability assumes independent, equally likely signs; shared data and metrics mean it should not be read as a probability of fabrication or as excluding every reporting error. The attainability checker was validated against 15 constructed confusion matrices and accepted all 15. Worst case: LSTM at 31 nodes reports accuracy 64.7% where its own DR and F1 imply 10.0%.

For a forecaster that reproduces a frozen value from frozen history, the residual vanishes. That explains a failure mode in the tested setup; it is not a proof that every possible forecaster must behave that way. A separate stuck-sensor rule performed well here.

3. Comparing trained models with simple rules

A composite of the two zero-parameter rules reaches F1 71.5 / ACC 76.9 / DR 60.5 at FA 7.9%, without fitted parameters or neural-training seed variation. This does not remove uncertainty from the sampled data. Trained models reach F1 ≈ 70 at a higher false-alarm rate when trained on benign windows only — and only 44–46 under the paper’s own literal prescription that every split carry equal samples per class. Under the paper’s stated training reading, every trained model scores below the zero-parameter composite. Every baseline the paper reports — SVM 6.1, RF 17.5, LGBM 19.8, FFNN 20.9, AE 21.5, LSTM 26.0 — falls below a detector with no parameters.

4. Does adding model capacity help?

The paper attributes its architecture to spatial and temporal structure being too difficult for a feed-forward network. Varying capacity across a 272× parameter range leaves performance flat:

Increasing capacity did not improve this tested comparison. That weakens a capacity-only explanation within these settings; it does not establish a universal capacity limit. Structure does exist (cross-sensor correlation 0.18–0.24, lag-one temporal correlation 0.65–0.74 on attack-free windows) — the premise is not empty. What fails is the inference that exploiting it requires this architecture.

5. Why replay was difficult in these tests

Replay reproduces genuine past readings, so a replayed window is a real benign window. Nearest-neighbour distance from held-out test windows to a bank of attack-free training windows:

ClassMedian distanceAUC vs normal
normal9.92—
replay9.750.455
denial of service16.310.917
manipulation1422.400.988

Replay was poorly detected by the tested rules. The 57.1% non-replay share is what total detection would reach if all non-replay attacks and no replay attacks were caught; it is not an unconditional detection ceiling. A formal indistinguishability bound would require equal benign/replay distributions over the detector’s available inputs and must account for its false-alarm rate. A nearest-neighbour AUC of 0.455 does not by itself establish that equality. The paper also leaves the attack mix unspecified.

6. Stability

Across five seeds on identical hardware, software and data, TGCN spans 23.7 F1 points and STGT 11.8; feed-forward and recurrent baselines stay within 2–8. STGT’s denial-of-service detection alone swings from 5.8% to 95.8%. The paper reports single values to one decimal with no dispersion.

7. Where the paper's premise holds

This result went against our initial expectation. The graph topology carries real signal. Shuffling the C-Town adjacency (same edges, permuted labels) or removing edges costs up to 18 F1: STGT at 10 nodes scores 57.9 real / 51.9 shuffled / 44.9 identity; TL-STGT at 31 nodes 63.6 / 45.2 / 44.7. The graph is not decorative.

Transfer learning shows a large gain at 31 nodes, +13.3 to +21.9 F1 over scratch STGT across seeds. Two caveats: the comparison is budget-confounded (TL-STGT is pretrained on the smaller graphs, so it sees roughly 3× the training), and under benign-only training the gain collapses from +21.9 to +3.1 — so the gain depends strongly on the training setup. This comparison does not isolate all effects of pretraining budget and training contamination.

8. The training setup changes the comparison

The paper states every split carries equal samples per class, so half the training data is attack-corrupted. Training on benign windows instead moves F1 at 31 nodes by roughly 25 points:

Corrections to this audit

These corrections are part of the evidence. Earlier conclusions changed when we found mistakes in our own analysis.

  1. Retracted — a reported 46-point shortfall against the paper. Substantially caused by a defect in our threshold selection, which drew reference samples from validation windows contaminated by adjacent attacks and inflated the threshold roughly 8,000×.
  2. Retracted — attributing an identical-metrics collapse to the scale incoherence of δ. Corrected runs show the threshold defect alone produced it.
  3. Corrected — a structure measurement reading ≈0 temporal correlation, which would have supported “there is no temporal structure.” It used attack-contaminated history; on attack-free windows the correlation is 0.67.
  4. Corrected — a nearest-neighbour test whose reference bank included the samples being evaluated, which matched themselves at distance zero and inverted the comparison.
  5. Corrected — an earlier draft of this page quoted benign-training results without saying so, while the paper’s literal reading trains on the balanced split. The two differ by ~25 F1. Every figure now carries its configuration.
  6. Corrected — this audit expected the graph topology to carry no signal. It does (§7). Reported against expectation.

Editorial clarification, 31 August 2026: the search range is finite; the sign-test assumptions are explicit; capacity results are limited to tested settings; and the replay observation is no longer presented as an unconditional impossibility proof. Measurements have not been changed. The earlier PDF and evidence entries retain their dated wording; see the appended correction in the evidence record.

Limitations

The benign series is the canonical DeepH2O C-Town series, not the paper’s own unpublished 1,400-hour run. Attack duration, replay offset, window length, and the shallow classifiers’ detection rule are not specified by the paper and are documented assumptions. The attack mix is ours, which conditions the ceiling in §5. The pre-registered interpretation sweep is incomplete.

This project assesses reproducibility within documented finite experiment spaces. It does not infer author intent. Source code and evidence are at github.com/fjoad/atk-evidence.
