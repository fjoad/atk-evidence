Reading the electricity-theft paper

This is the earlier account, not the current reproduction. The results below use older assumptions, including omitted test-set resampling. They are preserved for comparison. The current report includes the later FC-SAE experiment, output-domain and fitted-Sigmoid checks, and a separately bounded LSTM-SAE run on one V100 inside the paper's reported 183-minute training time. What happened in the earlier experiment?

Short version: using the named ISET/CER data but omitting test-set ADASYN, our earlier seed-11 model missed nearly three quarters of the attacks and falsely accused more than half of the normal examples. This one run is strong evidence of a mismatch, but it is not yet a paper-wide verdict.

Attacks detected81% → 26.18%paper → reproduced
False alarms15% → 58.22%paper → reproduced
Balanced accuracy83% → 33.98%paper → reproduced

What this run does establish

  • The fully recorded no-test-resampling interpretation does not reproduce the paper’s FC-SAE result.
  • Changing the frozen batch size from 32 to 512 did not rescue it: the two runs are nearly identical.
  • For this trained model, adding synthetic benign test examples cannot change its 26.18% detection rate on the already scored attack rows.
  • With that detection rate fixed, even an impossible perfect 0% false-alarm rate would cap balanced accuracy at 63.09%, below the paper’s 83%.

What it does not establish

  • This older run omitted full test-set ADASYN. That step was completed in the later, separately reported experiment.
  • It does not prove every reasonable missing hyperparameter or interpretation must fail; those require predeclared repeated experiments.
  • It does not prove fabrication, adaptive sample construction, or what private code the authors used.
  • It is one primary seed. A statistical reproduction conclusion requires the remaining frozen seeds and ambiguity envelope.
The exact conditional proof: the paper defines balanced accuracy as (DR + 100 − FA) / 2. Holding this model’s malicious predictions fixed gives (26.1807 + 100 − 0) / 2 = 63.0903% even under the best mathematically possible false-alarm rate. This proves what ADASYN cannot repair for this trained model; it does not prove that all other training completions are capped there.

Could a better cutoff recover 83%?

No, not for this score vector. We let the test labels choose the most favorable cutoff after the fact. In the paper’s score direction, the best balanced accuracy was only 50.00%. Reversing the direction—treating lower error as more suspicious—raised it to 66.26%, still far below 83%.

This is an intentionally optimistic diagnostic, not a valid evaluation protocol. It shows the gap is not merely that we copied 0.58 instead of a nearby threshold.

Why did the autoencoder behave this way?

ELI5: the paper first turns the input into numbers above and below zero, then tells the final Softmax layer that it may output only positive numbers that add up to one. That is like asking a printer with no minus sign to copy a page full of negative numbers.

The trained model’s reconstruction-error ranking was 0.99946 correlated with the ranking obtained by reconstructing every profile as zero. This motivated a separate output-layer comparison. It did not establish that the output layer was the only cause or authorize changing the completed run.

What is and is not mathematically impossible: test-set ADASYN alone cannot raise this trained model above 63.09% balanced accuracy, and Table V’s changing false-alarm values cannot come from one unchanged model, threshold, and benign set. But the paper’s headline 83% is not yet proven impossible for every reasonable training completion. That broader claim remains open. The current investigation is described in the new report.

When the printed method cannot execute

The audit does not quietly fix impossible wording and does not stop after pointing it out. Every such item follows the same visible evidence ladder:

1 · LITERAL FAILURE

Quote and locate the printed operation; preserve exactly why it cannot exist, calculate, or run.

2 · REASONABLE REPAIRS

Predeclare the smallest executable repair and every other materially defensible reading under separate stable IDs.

3 · EXECUTE + REPORT

Run every repair and show all seeds, failures, timings, and metrics beside the paper's target here and in the scientific PDF.

Interpretation rule: a repair is evidence about a reasonable executable completion of the text. It is never described as the authors' hidden implementation. The working hypothesis is that reasonable completions will not reproduce the paper, but that expectation cannot select the branches or the displayed results. A successful repair is reported as plainly as a non-match.
1

Acquire the two named datasets

Plain English: one dataset already says which customers stole electricity. The other contains normal electricity days, so the paper manufactures six different kinds of fake theft from each normal day.

1a · SGCC — real labels already exist

IN PAPER About 40,000 customers, one daily reading over roughly three years, with benign/malicious labels supplied by the dataset.

IN PAPER Use those labels directly. The paper does not apply the six synthetic ISET attacks to SGCC.

CONTRADICTION The source contains 1,034 daily features, while the model section specifies an input in R^48. No transformation from 1,034 to 48 is given.

1b · ISET/CER — benign data, attacks added

IN PAPER Roughly 3,000 residential meters, half-hour readings over about 1.5 years. Form one 48-value profile per meter-day.

IN PAPER Apply all six attack functions to every customer’s benign matrix, producing six malicious matrices.

AMBIGUOUS The official allocation contains 4,225 residential meters. The paper gives no rule for selecting “roughly 3,000,” so both all-residential and seeded-3,000 readings must be named.

1c · The six ISET attack transforms

f1 one fixed α∈[0.1,0.8] scales readings; the scope of “all samples” is undefined
f2 independent β(d,t)∈[0.1,0.8] scales each reading
f3 zero a randomly selected 4–24 hour interval
f4 replace every reading by that day’s mean
f5 multiply the day’s mean by independent β(d,t)
f6 reverse the 48 readings in time
PRINTED f3 Equation (3) uses tf = ti − tl, placing the final time before the start. The paper also mixes 24 hourly indices with 48 half-hour values. The literal expression is retained as non-executable. Executable interpretation branches use addition plus clipping, addition plus wraparound, or redraw to a valid endpoint, crossed with declared hour-to-half-hour mappings; these were candidate interpretations, not a statement that every branch had been executed.
2

Prepare two different learning problems

Plain English: the anomaly models study only normal days, like learning what a healthy heartbeat looks like. They are then tested on normal and altered days. The paper also changes the test set itself by inventing extra examples with ADASYN.

2a · Anomaly detectors

normalize B + M→split benign 2:1→train on B1 only→test = B2 + all M→ADASYN on test

IN PAPER This is the paper’s order. In particular, it explicitly oversamples the test set, even though that is statistically questionable.

AMBIGUOUS “Zero mean and unit variance” does not identify the fit population or axis: joint B+M, per class, per profile, or benign training only.

2b · Supervised detectors

combine B + M for all customers→ADASYN→split 2:1→same feature scaling

IN PAPER ADASYN appears before the supervised train/test split.

CONTRADICTION The paper first requires unseen test customers, but later describes row/sample splitting after synthesis. Both cannot be assumed true without an explicit identity policy.

2c · ADASYN — “invent more examples from the smaller group”

IN PAPER For ISET, the paper applies ADASYN after building the anomaly-detector test set. In this earlier construction that test set has 750,770 benign profiles and 13,507,740 attack profiles, so benign is the smaller group.

ELI5: ADASYN looks at each smaller-group example, finds its nearest neighbors, and draws new synthetic examples between nearby smaller-group points. It does not inspect which neural model failed, and it cannot manufacture examples specially chosen for the next model.

Historical estimate—not the current runtime: the selected exact default implementation must compare roughly 750,770 × 14,258,510 = 10.7 trillion profile pairs in its first neighbor search, then another 0.56 trillion pairs in a smaller-group search and generate about 12.8 million synthetic benign profiles. A same-machine 16-core benchmark extrapolates to about 14.2 wall-hours for the two neighbor searches alone, before synthesis and disk output. A prior bounded attempt ran for 79 minutes and consumed 9.35 CPU-hours without completing the first search.
What the paper does not tell us: it names ADASYN but gives no library, neighbor-search implementation, approximation rule, hardware, subsample, random seed, or preprocessing time. Exact GPU search or approximate neighbors could be much faster than our CPU default. Fourteen CPU hours is also entirely possible as an overnight preprocessing job. Therefore the audit can report the cost of this declared default and the missing implementation details; it cannot honestly claim the authors “could not have run ADASYN” or infer what they actually ran.

STATISTICAL CAUTION Applying oversampling to an evaluation set is unusual: it changes the exam after the model is trained. Here it adds benign rows only. It may change false alarms, AUC, precision, and balanced accuracy, but it cannot change detection on the existing malicious rows.

About model-tailored synthetic samples: standard ADASYN uses class labels and nearest neighbors, not the neural model’s successes or failures. With benign as the smaller class, it creates more synthetic benign profiles near places where attack profiles are common. Running it again may produce a slightly different random test set, but it does not automatically create examples specially chosen for FC-SAE, then LSTM-SAE, and so on. The paper reports no model-adaptive sampling procedure.

2d · Corrected control run beside the reproduction

ALONGSIDE Keep customers disjoint; fit imputation and scaling on training data only; synthesize/oversample training data only; never alter validation or test rows. This answers whether a conventional leakage-free detector works. It does not rescue or replace the paper-literal result.

3

Build the benchmark and autoencoder families

Plain English: an autoencoder is a copy machine trained on normal days. If it struggles to copy a new day, the paper calls that day suspicious. The five models are different kinds of copy machines.

3a · Detector families

ANOMALY ARIMA, one-class SVM, FC-SAE, LSTM-SAE, FC-VAE, LSTM-VAE, and LSTM-AEA.

SUPERVISED Naive Bayes, “multiclass” SVM, feed-forward neural network, and supervised LSTM.

Repeat-latent, zero-after-first-step, and autoregressive decoder completions—and both merge readings—must remain named executable interpretations.
4

Select hyperparameters and anomaly thresholds

Plain English: the paper lists the winning machine settings and the score that triggers an alarm, but it omits much of the recipe needed to reproduce how those winners and alarm cutoffs were selected.

4a · Algorithm 6

IN PAPER On ISET training data, search sequentially over layer count, neuron count, optimizer, dropout, hidden activation, and output activation.

INCOMPLETE Epochs, batch size, learning rate, initialization, seeds, repetitions, final refit, and a usable convergence rule are absent. The scalar width loop also does not directly produce Table I’s unequal widths.

4b · Thresholds

IN PAPER The paper prints 0.58, 0.61, 0.43, 0.47, 0.51 for FC-SAE, LSTM-SAE, FC-VAE, LSTM-VAE, and LSTM-AEA.

NON-UNIQUE It says thresholds come from the “median of the IQR of the ROC curve.” That phrase does not define one algorithm. ROC selection also requires attack labels, while X_TR is defined as benign-only.

TIMING LIMIT Table IV reports 137–193 minutes, but no hardware, software versions, epoch count, batch size, repetition count, or timing boundary. We can report our measured time; we cannot test equality to those minutes on an unknown machine/procedure.
5

Evaluate once, then populate Tables II–V

Plain English: every profile receives one suspiciousness score. A fixed cutoff turns that score into “normal” or “attack,” and all reported metrics should then come from the same predictions and test population.

5a · Metrics from one score vector and confusion matrix

IN PAPER Detection rate, false-alarm rate, specificity, precision, F1, ROC-AUC, and balanced accuracy—called “ACC” in the paper: (DR + specificity) / 2.

REPORTED-METRIC ARITHMETIC The precision prose describes recall although the printed formula is TP/(TP+FP). Table-II Naive Bayes reports DR=75, PR=75, and F1=77, although the stated harmonic-mean formula forces F1=75. More broadly, the DR/FA/PR rows in neither Table II nor Table III admit one common test prevalence even with generous rounding. These static contradictions are shown beside, but are not substituted for, experimental reproduction results.

The paper reports different FA values. Therefore at least one experiment identity—model fit, benign sample, threshold, or another unreported element—must vary. Common-model/common-benign, per-attack retraining, per-attack benign resampling, and their combination are separate declared readings.

Where this leaves the earlier notes

The old execution sequence is superseded. One expensive run per model is not a cheap first pass. The current work uses small checks to identify which explanations deserve another full experiment.

The later FC-SAE run used a newly documented reading, a different seed, all residential customers, attacks only from held-out customers, and completed test-set ADASYN. It reached 25.48% detection and 40.18% balanced accuracy. A subsequent Softmax-domain proof excludes the target under those fixed data/output/score assumptions; a small trained Sigmoid control also failed every cutoff on its sampled evaluation, while longer Sigmoid training remains unresolved. Those results and their limits belong to the new reproduction report, not this earlier run.

Original results, code, and changes of interpretation remain in the research record. No additional training is authorized by this page.
